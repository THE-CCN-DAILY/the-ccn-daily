# Project Phoenix: Product Doctrine

## Core Mission
Project Phoenix is a spiritual formation platform designed to foster deep Bible engagement, consistent habit loops, and community accountability. Our goal is to facilitate spiritual growth through structured learning and meaningful connection.

## The Five Pillars
1. **Bible Formation Engine**: Deep, contextual engagement with Scripture.
2. **Habit/Discipleship Loop**: Consistent daily practices that build spiritual character.
3. **Courses & Challenges**: Structured learning paths with cohort-based accountability.
4. **Library Stack**: A curated collection of books, audiobooks, and resources for lifelong learning.
5. **Community & Mentorship**: Safe spaces for sharing, prayer, and mutual growth.

## AI Role Hierarchy
AI is an enabler and facilitator, not the core product identity. It serves to enhance the human experience, not replace it.

### Tier A: Invisible AI
- **Role**: Infrastructure and background optimization.
- **Examples**: Content tagging, search indexing, personalized recommendations.
- **Goal**: Seamlessly improve the user experience without being noticed.

### Tier B: Contextual Assist
- **Role**: Providing immediate help within a specific context.
- **Examples**: "Explain this verse", "Summarize this chapter", "Define this theological term".
- **Goal**: Lower the barrier to understanding complex content.

### Tier C: Optional Generation
- **Role**: Creative and generative tasks that the user explicitly triggers.
- **Examples**: Personalized devotionals, AI-generated prayer topics, cinematic backgrounds.
- **Goal**: Provide inspiration and unique perspectives when requested.

## Non-Negotiables
- **No AI-First Global UX**: The primary interface must always be human-centric and focused on the Five Pillars.
- **No Theological Overreach**: AI should provide information and perspectives, but never claim spiritual authority or replace pastoral guidance.
- **No Spammy Prompts**: AI interactions must be high-quality, relevant, and respectful of the user's focus.

## Evaluation Rubric for New Features
Every new feature must be evaluated against these questions:
1. Does it directly support one of the Five Pillars?
2. Is the AI role appropriate for the task (Tier A, B, or C)?
3. Does it maintain the human-centric focus of the platform?
4. Is the cost of the AI operation justified by the user value provided?
